Before You Buy.

VHS maxes out around 480i. No converter makes tape HD — anything promising “1080p capture” is upscaling, not resolution.
You still need a working VCR. Every capture device assumes a deck that tracks well. Most failures we see are VCR failures.
Capture is real-time. A two-hour tape takes two hours. Forty tapes is a month of evenings.
RCA vs S-Video matters. S-Video skips one round of signal mixing — visibly cleaner if your deck supports it.
Watch for audio drift. Cheap dongles slowly desync audio on long tapes — check your first capture at the 90-minute mark.
Moldy or sticky tapes: stop. A consumer device (and your VCR) can’t handle them — playing a moldy tape can destroy tape and deck both.

DIY vs. a Pro Service.

DIY is genuinely fine if…
You have a few tapes, not a closet
Your VCR works and tracks well
The tapes are healthy — no mold, no squeal
You have the evenings and the patience
Use a pro if…
The tapes are irreplaceable
There are many of them
Any are moldy, sticky, or damaged
You want 4K output and cleanup
You don’t own a working VCR
Your time is worth more than the savings
